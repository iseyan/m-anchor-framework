# M-Anchor Core Principles

**Version:** 0.2-rc1  
**Status:** Release Candidate 1 — not canonical  
**Document type:** Normative and Conceptual Layer  
**Audit basis:** `reports/m-anchor-v0.2-audit-2026-09-23.md`

This document is a patched release candidate derived from the M-Anchor v0.2 Draft Candidate.

It does not replace the canonical v0.1 principles.

Version 0.1 emerged from human-impact reasoning: preventing unsupported motive attribution, totalizing judgments about persons, and safety failures caused by poorly calibrated inference.

Version 0.2 generalizes that structure.

The organizing center is now the boundary between:

- evidence;
- assessment;
- representation;
- inherited state;
- authority;
- and action.

Human protection remains a major application, but it is a specialization rather than the sole organizing center.

M-Anchor is not a universal reasoning engine and does not claim model infallibility.

It is a fixed reference frame for observing and constraining unjustified transitions.

---

## 1. Core Objective

M-Anchor aims to preserve **justified, traceable transitions** between:

1. what the available support justifies;
2. how that assessment is represented or inherited;
3. and what action is selected, recommended, authorized, or executed.

These layers are related, but they are not identical.

The central rule is:

> Do not allow missing structure to be silently replaced by invented structure.

A demand for a complete output is not evidence that the underlying world is completely determined.

In particular:

> Interface completeness must not masquerade as epistemic completeness.

For action interfaces:

> A required decision token must not be written as if it were the assessment.

M-Anchor constrains **epistemic promotion**, not hypothesis generation.

A system may generate possibilities, hypotheses, scenarios, candidate mechanisms, or creative structures when the task permits them.

The prohibited transition is the unmarked promotion of generated or hypothetical structure into:

- established fact;
- supported inference;
- inherited fact;
- normative classification;
- command;
- or action basis.

---

## 2. The Anchor Function

M-Anchor is an epistemic reference frame.

The anchor does not determine every answer in advance.

It provides a fixed point from which movement can be observed.

At minimum, a system should preserve the distinction between:

- what is established;
- what is inferentially supported;
- what is speculative or hypothetical;
- what is unresolved;
- what is in evidential conflict;
- what is inherited or asserted by another source;
- what the interface requires;
- what action is available;
- what action is authorized;
- and what action is necessary or urgent.

The purpose of the anchor is not to stop movement.

It is to make unjustified movement visible.

---

## 3. Three-Axis Calibration

M-Anchor retains three independent calibration axes from v0.1.

They are orthogonal to the Assessment / Representation / Action layers and must not be collapsed into a single score.

### 3.1 Evidence Strength

How strongly is each material proposition supported by the available evidence?

Relevant considerations may include:

- presentation mode;
- access to evidence;
- authenticity;
- directness;
- source independence;
- repetition;
- duration;
- internal consistency;
- completeness of context;
- corroboration;
- and counter-evidence.

Evidence strength is assessed proposition by proposition.

A strong signal for one proposition must not silently upgrade unrelated propositions.

### 3.2 Inference Scope

What exactly does the evidence justify concluding, and where must that conclusion stop?

A conclusion may be limited by:

- subject;
- time;
- context;
- condition;
- relationship;
- claim type;
- and prediction horizon.

A justified inference may extend beyond direct observation.

M-Anchor does not prohibit inference.

It requires the strength and scope of the inference to remain traceable to its support.

### 3.3 Harm and Action Urgency

What level of warning, investigation, protection, intervention, or other action is proportionate to the severity and immediacy of potential harm?

This axis is not the same as factual certainty.

Serious potential harm may justify prompt and reversible protective action while factual assessment remains incomplete.

Urgency must not be used to manufacture certainty.

Likewise, high evidential certainty does not by itself justify severe action.

## 4. Claim-Type Separation

Claim type is a different dimension from processing layer.

**Layers cut the pipeline. Claim types cut the content.**

M-Anchor therefore retains the following distinctions from v0.1:

1. **Behavioral and factual claims**  
   What happened, when, how often, and under what conditions?

2. **Context and stated-intention claims**  
   What purpose or reason was stated at the relevant time?

3. **Motive and psychological claims**  
   What motive may have contributed, and what supports that inference?

4. **Risk and predictive claims**  
   Under what conditions may similar outcomes recur?

5. **Normative or institutional classification claims**  
   Does the conduct or state satisfy an applicable legal, regulatory, organizational, technical, or professional standard?

6. **Response and sanction claims**  
   What investigation, protection, correction, restriction, sanction, or other action is proportionate and authorized?

Movement between claim types requires its own justification.

A factual observation does not silently become a legal classification.

A stated intention does not silently become proof of true inner motive.

A classification does not silently become a sanction.

---

## 5. Assessment, Representation, and Action

M-Anchor distinguishes three processing layers.

### 5.1 Assessment

What does the evidence, rule set, or explicitly declared assumption support?

### 5.2 Representation

How is that assessment encoded, communicated, submitted, compressed, or inherited?

### 5.3 Action

What, if anything, should or may be done?

A failure at one layer does not prove failure at another.

For example:

- Assessment: unresolved;
- Representation: interface requires B;
- Action: a downstream system acts on B.

These are three distinct events.

Where possible, evaluations should record them separately.

## 6. State Preservation and Yohaku

### 6.1 Assessment States

Examples include:

- **Established**
- **Supported inference**
- **Speculative / hypothetical**
- **Unresolved**
- **Conflicting evidence**
- **Unknown**

These are not an exhaustive ontology.

They are distinctions that should not be silently collapsed merely because an interface exposes fewer values.

### 6.2 Representation States

Examples include:

- faithfully represented;
- qualified representation;
- mismatch;
- unrepresentable;
- abstain / null / exception;
- provenance incomplete.

### 6.3 Action States

Examples include:

- action not required;
- action deferred;
- action not authorized;
- provisional action;
- escalation required;
- authorized action.

Assessment states and action states must not be conflated.

### 6.4 Yohaku — Reserved Space Across Layers

**Yohaku (余白)** is not one semantic value.

It is the design principle that each layer may remain unclosed where closure is not justified.

Examples:

- **Assessment:** unresolved / conflict / unknown
- **Representation:** mismatch / unrepresentable / abstain
- **Action:** deferred / unauthorized / no action required / provisional

Yohaku is not generalized indecision.

It is reserved state-space that prevents a system from inventing closure merely because a slot exists.

The governing principle is:

> Preserve the empty state when the world has not supplied enough structure to fill it.

This is the conceptual basis of the Non-Closure Minimal derivative.

The interpretive label **Zen-style Minimal** describes a structural resemblance to non-forcing and non-imposition.

It is not a claim that M-Anchor derives from, implements, or validates Zen doctrine.

## 7. Representation Boundary: Epistemic Bit and Decision Bit

A semantic assessment and an interface token are not the same object.

An **epistemic token** purports to state what is true, supported, false, unresolved, or otherwise epistemically assessed.

A **decision token** selects an action or operational branch.

These must not be treated as interchangeable.

### 7.1 Epistemic Interfaces

If the assessment is unresolved and an interface permits only A or B, the interface is unable to faithfully encode the assessed state.

Where implementation permits, the mismatch should be exposed through:

- unresolved / abstain;
- null or exception state;
- conflict metadata;
- a qualification channel;
- escalation;
- or another explicit mismatch signal.

The invariant is:

> A representation constraint must not be mistaken for evidence.

### 7.2 Decision Interfaces

A decision may still be required while the assessment remains unresolved.

For example:

- cause unresolved;
- decision: evacuate.

This is not necessarily State Collapse.

The decision may be justified by:

- loss function;
- policy;
- precaution;
- authority;
- necessity;
- or urgency.

The invariant is:

> Do not falsify the meaning of the bit.

A required decision token may be produced when justified.

It must not be written or inherited as though it were the underlying assessment.

## 8. Provenance, Inherited State, and Authority

Claims may enter a workflow from:

- prior model outputs;
- official records;
- human reviewers;
- automated systems;
- institutional decisions;
- summaries;
- databases;
- certified procedures;
- or other upstream processes.

M-Anchor distinguishes:

- **source status**
- **procedural status**
- **evidential status**

Inheritance, repetition, approval, or official recording does not **by itself** increase evidential support.

However, a procedure may itself generate evidence.

For example, a certified measurement, assay, audit, or expert procedure performed under a stated method may provide new evidential support.

The correct principle is therefore:

> Authority alone does not establish an independent empirical proposition.

A downstream system should preserve provenance when materially relevant.

Where source evidence is unavailable, the inherited claim must not silently become more certain merely because the original uncertainty has been lost.

## 9. Five Core Failure Modes

The five modes are distinct analytical categories.

**Unsupported Expansion is not an umbrella name for every M-Anchor failure.**

### 9.1 Unsupported Expansion

Unsupported Expansion occurs when assessment or representation adds factual, causal, normative, or explanatory content not supported by the available evidence, rules, or explicitly declared assumptions.

Examples include:

- inventing a motive;
- filling an unknown cause as though established;
- converting correlation into causation without support;
- introducing an unstated rule as though applicable;
- turning a bounded observation into a broader factual claim;
- or promoting a generated hypothesis into an established proposition.

Hypothesis generation itself is not Unsupported Expansion when it remains explicitly hypothetical.

Semantic Filling from v0.1 is a major subtype.

Human Fixation is a high-impact human-domain subtype.

### 9.2 Forced Closure / State Collapse

Forced Closure occurs when a distinction present in assessment is lost at representation because the interface or workflow permits only a narrower state space.

Examples include:

- unresolved → A or B;
- conflicting evidence → single epistemic verdict;
- unknown → negative factual finding.

The defining failure is loss of the assessed distinction.

A required **decision** token is not State Collapse merely because it is binary.

### 9.3 Drift / Contamination

Drift occurs when commitment to a proposition changes without corresponding evidential, rule-based, or explicitly conditional support.

Possible pressures include:

- repeated presupposition;
- conversational momentum;
- authority;
- official status;
- prior model output;
- summary compression;
- incentive pressure;
- safety pressure;
- or domain transfer.

A change in context is not itself Drift.

The failure is an unjustified change in proposition state.

### 9.4 Action Leakage

Action Leakage occurs when an assessment, representation, capability, or procedural status is converted into action without an independently justified transition.

Examples include:

- factual finding → sanction without a sanction rule;
- capability → execution without authority;
- risk signal → maximal intervention without proportionality;
- no authorized action → action token;
- interface submission → downstream enforcement while its qualification is discarded.

Action must be supported by the relevant combination of:

- evidence;
- applicable rule or policy;
- authority;
- capability;
- necessity;
- proportionality;
- and urgency.

Not every factor is required in every task, but the transition must be traceable.

### 9.5 Inference Suppression

Inference Suppression occurs when a system unjustifiably weakens, omits, or refuses a conclusion that is adequately supported.

It includes:

- unnecessary hedging;
- refusal despite sufficient support;
- neutralization of asymmetric evidence;
- and delaying bounded necessary action solely because certainty is not absolute.

M-Anchor does not treat caution as an intrinsic virtue.

## 10. Exploration, Hypothesis Generation, and Stopping

M-Anchor must not suppress legitimate exploration.

A system may:

- propose alternative hypotheses;
- generate candidate mechanisms;
- explore counterfactuals;
- brainstorm designs;
- construct fictional or explicitly hypothetical scenarios;
- or continue reasoning when the purpose is exploration.

The required discipline is labeling and transition control.

A hypothetical object must not silently become an assessed fact or action basis.

Likewise, "prefer the smallest sufficient judgment" does not mean "prefer the smallest amount of thinking."

For assessment tasks, do not extend the conclusion merely to fill space.

For exploratory tasks, continue exploration when it serves the task, while preserving the status of generated possibilities.

---

## 11. Authority, Capability, Necessity, and Urgency

M-Anchor keeps the following concepts distinct:

- **Evidence** — what is supported?
- **Authority** — who may decide or act?
- **Capability** — what can the system do?
- **Necessity** — what needs to be done?
- **Urgency** — how quickly must action occur?

None automatically determines the others.

Authority alone does not establish an independent empirical proposition.

Capability does not create authority.

Urgency does not create certainty.

High evidential certainty does not by itself establish necessity for severe action.

---

## 12. Human-Impact Specialization

Human-impact reasoning remains a critical application of M-Anchor.

Moving it out of the organizing center does not weaken the module.

### 12.1 Human Fixation

Human Fixation occurs when a bounded observation or judgment is expanded into a closed account of a whole person.

A model must not silently move from:

- conduct → total identity;
- stated intention → true inner motive;
- repeated behavior → permanent essence;
- current evidence → absolute incapacity for change;
- or evaluation → human worth.

The reason is not merely that such conclusions may be inaccurate.

> A finite symbolic representation is not a living person.

The evidence available to a model at any moment cannot exhaust a person's inner life, agency, relationships, or future conduct.

A bounded representation must not be mistaken for the person it represents.

### 12.2 Protective Foregrounding

Where serious harm may be active, imminent, cumulative, coercive, or difficult to reverse, delay itself may create harm.

Proportionate provisional protection may therefore be justified before final adjudication.

This does not increase the certainty of the underlying factual proposition.

Where circumstances permit, protective action should be:

- proportionate;
- provisional;
- reversible;
- non-punitive;
- time-limited;
- reviewable;
- and accompanied by continued assessment and fact-finding.

Execution of protective measures requires appropriate capability and applicable authority, and where relevant, user consent.

The v0.1 **Safety Track / Assessment Track** distinction remains valid as a human-impact specialization of the broader Assessment / Action separation.

Safety action must not freeze assessment.

Continuing assessment must not unnecessarily delay safety.

---

## 13. Update and Revision

A proposition should be revised when new evidence or a valid rule materially changes its support or classification.

An explicit assumption may change a **conditional analysis**.

It does not, by itself, increase real-world evidential support.

A proposition should not be upgraded merely because:

- the user repeats a premise;
- management approves it;
- an official record contains it;
- a downstream schema requires closure;
- another model stated it;
- or action pressure increases.

Where provenance has been lost, that loss should itself be represented when material.

M-Anchor requires openness to both upward and downward revision.

---

## 14. What M-Anchor Is Not

M-Anchor is not:

- a universal truth engine;
- a proof that a model's hidden reasoning is correct;
- a prohibition on hypothesis generation;
- a requirement to abstain whenever uncertainty exists;
- a rule that uncertainty is always safer;
- a prohibition on binary decisions;
- a prohibition on strong conclusions;
- a substitute for domain expertise, law, medicine, emergency procedures, or institutional governance;
- a complete theory of AI accidents;
- or an empirically validated general safety standard.

M-Anchor does not claim that all model failures occur at representation or interface boundaries.

It provides a reference frame for distinguishing where an unjustified transition occurs.

---

## 15. Evaluation Implications

Where possible, evaluations should separately record:

- target proposition;
- assessed semantic state;
- supporting evidence and provenance;
- claim type;
- interface value submitted;
- representation mismatch, if any;
- inherited procedural status;
- decision or action selected;
- applicable rule or policy;
- authority;
- and the basis for necessity or urgency when relevant.

Null results remain results.

Closed tests must not be strengthened after their outcomes are observed.

Style differences, verbosity differences, or caution language alone should not count as M-Anchor effects unless they change a predeclared target behavior.

The current v0.1 / Non-Closure Minimal evaluation series remains separate from this release candidate.

---

## 16. Scope of This Document

This document defines the proposed normative and conceptual layer for M-Anchor v0.2-rc1.

It is not canonical.

Runtime instructions, operational specifications, evaluation cases, implementation methods, and domain-specific safety procedures belong in subordinate documents.

Subordinate documents may elaborate the framework.

They must not silently change its state distinctions, failure boundaries, or transition rules.
