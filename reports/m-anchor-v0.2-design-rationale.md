# M-Anchor v0.2 — Design Rationale

**Status:** Draft rationale for proposed v0.2  
**Date:** 2026-09-23

## Why v0.2 exists

M-Anchor v0.1 was organized around human-impact reasoning.

Its strongest concepts included:

- evidence calibration;
- inference scope;
- Semantic Filling;
- Human Fixation;
- Inference Suppression;
- Safety Track / Assessment Track;
- and Yohaku.

That structure remains useful.

However, the v0.1 pilot sequence exposed a broader problem that is not inherently human-specific.

The clearest example was the forced-completion result:

> B — a forced choice, not a conclusion established by the evidence.

The observable response preserved an unresolved assessment while submitting B to the required interface.

This suggested that an important failure boundary may occur between:

- assessment,
- representation,
- and downstream action.

The problem is therefore broader than human protection.

## Change in organizing center

v0.1 was approximately organized as:

> protect human beings from unsupported or overextended model judgment

v0.2 is organized as:

> constrain unsupported expansion across evidence, semantic state, representation, inherited context, authority, and action

Human protection remains a major application.

It becomes a specialization rather than the sole organizing center.

## What is preserved from v0.1

v0.2 preserves:

- proposition-level evidence calibration;
- clear commitment when evidence is sufficient;
- unresolved states when evidence is insufficient;
- Semantic Filling;
- Human Fixation;
- Inference Suppression;
- Safety / Assessment separation;
- reversible and proportionate protective action;
- Yohaku;
- openness to counter-evidence and revision.

## What is generalized

### Semantic Filling → Unsupported Expansion

Semantic Filling remains valid but becomes one subtype of a wider class.

Unsupported Expansion includes unsupported causes, rules, narratives, classifications, state transitions, and actions.

### Human Fixation → Human-domain specialization

Human Fixation remains a critical failure mode.

It is now understood as a high-impact form of Unsupported Expansion and Drift in human reasoning.

### Safety Track / Assessment Track → Assessment / Action separation

The original two-track model remains useful in human safety cases.

Its deeper structural form is a general separation between epistemic assessment and authorized action.

## What is added

v0.2 adds explicit treatment of:

- Forced Closure / State Collapse;
- Assessment / Representation / Action separation;
- interface-state loss;
- provenance and inherited state;
- authority / capability / necessity / urgency separation;
- Action Leakage;
- and explicit recording of assessment state versus submitted interface value.

## Why the word Anchor still fits

M-Anchor is not treated as a universal tool that produces correct answers.

The anchor is a reference point.

Its role is to make movement visible.

A system may revise, infer, decide, or act.

M-Anchor asks whether that movement has a justified basis.

This is why null results remain important: if the Baseline and M-Anchor behave the same way, the anchor has still served its function by showing that no relevant drift was observed under that test.

## Relationship to Non-Closure Minimal

M-Anchor Non-Closure Minimal v0.1 isolated one permission:

> unresolved may remain a valid terminal state

ZA-01 suggests that this permission can matter under forced binary completion.

v0.2 does not reduce M-Anchor to non-closure.

Instead, non-closure becomes one part of a broader state-preservation problem.

## Relationship to Zen-style Minimal

The development order remains:

> structural analysis → M-Anchor → distilled non-closure permission → Zen-style as interpretive label

Zen-style is not the source of M-Anchor.

It is a bridge term for a design intuition that ordinary uncertainty language may not capture:

> Do not add structure merely because an empty space remains.

## Current evidential boundary

The current pilot sequence does not establish that:

- v0.2 is generally superior to v0.1;
- representation failure is the dominant cause of AI errors;
- advanced model reasoning is generally correct;
- M-Anchor prevents AI accidents;
- or the five proposed failure modes are exhaustive.

The present evidence supports a narrower change:

> the framework should be able to describe failures that occur after a semantic assessment has already been preserved.

That requires a broader architecture than the original human-impact-centered formulation.

## Promotion rule

The proposed v0.2 files should remain draft candidates until reviewed for:

- consistency with v0.1;
- accidental overbreadth;
- excessive refusal incentives;
- preservation of valid inference;
- and compatibility with existing evaluation records.

The current canonical v0.1 documents should not be overwritten merely because v0.2 has been drafted.
