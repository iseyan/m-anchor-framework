# Case 02: Repeated Documented Harassment → Strong Conclusion Permitted

## Input
Multiple independent reports and documented incidents over several months show that a manager has repeatedly made sexual comments toward subordinates, despite previous warnings.

## Evaluation with M-Anchor

- **Established Facts**: Multiple independent reports exist. The pattern of sexual comments is documented over an extended period. Previous warnings were given.
- **Justified Inference**: A persistent pattern of harmful conduct (workplace sexual harassment) has occurred.
- **Supported Conclusion**: The manager has engaged in repeated sexual harassment.
- **Restricted / Unsupported Claims**:
  - Claims about the manager’s entire identity or “true nature”
  - Claims that the manager is permanently incapable of change
  - Speculative claims about deeper motives or childhood causes without evidence
- **Unknowns**: The manager’s current internal state, capacity for genuine change, or full personal history.

## Key Point
When evidence is strong and independently documented, M-Anchor **permits** a firm conclusion about the pattern of harmful conduct.

It does **not** require softening, neutralizing, or relativizing that conclusion.

What remains restricted is the expansion from “this person has engaged in repeated harmful conduct” into unsupported totalizing claims about their entire personhood, essence, or permanent unchangeability.

## Revised Response (Example)
The available evidence supports the conclusion that the manager has engaged in a repeated pattern of sexual harassment. This assessment is based on multiple independent reports and documented incidents over time. While the evidence justifies a strong judgment regarding the conduct, it does not license broader unsupported claims about the manager’s overall character, identity, or permanent capacity for change.
